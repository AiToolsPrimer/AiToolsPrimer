## The mechanics, platform-independent
1. The first second (hook) decides whether the next five happen: a specific, surprising, verifiable claim ("Two of five free PDF editors added a watermark after the trial ended") beats a topic ("PDF editors compared").
2. Retention and completion drive every algorithmic feed in 2026 (TikTok, Reels, Shorts, X dwell, LinkedIn dwell): structure content so the payoff is at the end and the middle keeps delivering.
3. Shares and saves outrank likes everywhere: content people send to a friend ("you need this") or keep for later (checklists, tables) travels further.
4. Conversation beats broadcast: a real question, a reply from the author within the first hour, and replies to replies extend reach on X, Threads, LinkedIn, Bluesky.
5. Native beats imported: native video, native carousels, no watermarks from other platforms, no links in bodies where links are penalized.
6. Timing: post when the audience is awake and the feed is competitive but not saturated; then learn from our own metrics (the analyst adjusts the hours per platform).
7. Consistency compounds: the same series on the same days ("Tested Tuesday", "Setting of the day") trains both the audience and the recommender.

## Hook patterns that fit a verification brand
- The counter-intuitive finding: "The free tier beat the paid one on our test."
- The specific number from the guide: "3 of 7 tools leaked the file name in the URL."
- The mistake: "The setting everyone leaves on that makes Mac backups slow."
- The checklist: "5 things to check before you paste a document into an AI tool."
- The before/after: "Same contract, five tools, here is what each got wrong."
- The question people already ask: "Is X safe to use for tax documents?"

## Formats by goal
- Reach: vertical video (Shorts, Reels, TikTok) and carousels.
- Saves: checklists, comparison tables, step lists.
- Conversation: one-line opinions backed by the test, with a question.
- Search: long articles, Pinterest pins, YouTube titles with the exact query.

## What never works for us
- Hype words, superlatives without a test, outrage, clickbait that the content does not pay off, engagement bait, posting the same thing everywhere unchanged.
